BEFORE YOU BEGIN

How to use this workbook.

This isn’t a worksheet to rush through. It’s a conversation with yourself — the one you’ve been too busy to have. A few gentle ground rules:

  1. Make it sacred

    Find twenty quiet minutes that belong only to you. Lock the door if you have to. This time is yours.

  2. Be honest, not aspirational

    Answer how things actually are — not how they should be, not how they’ll look to anyone else. Nobody’s grading this but grace.

  3. Keep a pen close

    The writing is where the shift happens. Don’t just think your answers — type them here or put them on paper where you can see them.

  4. Come back every season

    Your cup changes. So should your check-in. Return to these pages whenever you feel the dryness creeping back.

THE TRUTH

You can’t pour from an empty cup.

You already knew that. But knowing it and living it are two different things.

So you kept giving — from a cup nobody was refilling. You called it loyalty. You called it being strong. You called it just how I am. And it cost you. Your peace. Your patience. Your spark. The version of you that used to laugh easy and dream loud.

This isn’t about blame. It’s about noticing. Because the moment you see where you’ve been leaking — your energy, your love, your worth — is the same moment you get to do something about it.

An empty cup isn’t a flaw to fix.It’s an invitation to refill.

WHAT RUNNING ON EMPTY LOOKS LIKE

The slow leak nobody warns you about.

Empty rarely arrives all at once. It’s a slow leak — one yes too many, one skipped meal, one more night you put yourself last. Until one day you’re pouring from a cup that’s been dry far longer than you’d ever admit out loud.

And here’s the tricky part: you get good at running on empty. You learn to smile through the tired. You learn to perform “fine.” You become so used to the leak that you stop noticing it’s there — you just feel the slow fade of a woman who’s giving more than she’s getting.

That leak shows up in four places. Your body. Your heart. Your mind. Your spirit. That’s exactly where we’re about to look — gently, one area at a time.

You can’t fix what you won’t face. So let’s face it — gently, honestly, together.

02

PART TWO

The Reset

Before you can pour back in, one belief has to go: the lie that filling your own cup is selfish.

It isn’t. It never was. A woman who refuses to refill doesn’t become more loving — she becomes more depleted, more resentful, more gone. You were never meant to be a fountain that runs itself dry for everyone else’s comfort.

Refilling is not selfish. Refilling is the assignment. When your cup is full, your overflow blesses everyone near you — without costing you yourself.

Say it plain: You are allowed to take up space. You are allowed to need things. You are allowed to choose yourself — and call it holy.

03

PART THREE

Pour Back Into You

Five small, doable rituals to start refilling — today. You don’t need a free weekend. You need to begin. Check the ones you’re starting this week.

  1. Fill your cup first

    Before you check a single notification or answer a single need, give yourself ten quiet minutes that belong to no one but you. You go first now.

  2. Let “no” be a full sentence

    Every yes you give from empty is a no to yourself. A boundary isn’t rejection — it’s devotion to your own wholeness.

  3. Name what you need — out loud

    You’ve spent years reading everyone else’s needs. Turn that gift inward. You can’t receive what you won’t admit you want.

  4. Sit still with God

    Not to ask for anything. Just to be filled — and reminded who you are when you’re quiet enough to hear it.

  5. Pour into what pours back

    Notice the people and places that leave you fuller, not emptier — and lean all the way in.
