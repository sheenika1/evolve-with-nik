MODULE 1

Naming It: getting honest about where you are

Before you can leave a place, you have to admit you are standing in it.

Toxic relationships rarely announce themselves. They erode you slowly — a comment here, a broken promise there, a slow shrinking of who you used to be. You do not need permission, and you do not need it to get worse. You are allowed to leave a relationship simply because it is hurting you.

UNDERSTANDToxic vs. abusive

Signs of a toxic relationship

  • You feel smaller, more anxious, or less like yourself than you used to.
  • Constant criticism, contempt, or being made to feel “too much” or “never enough.”
  • Broken trust on repeat — lies, betrayal, or promises that never hold.
  • You walk on eggshells, manage their moods, and shrink to keep the peace.
  • Your needs, faith, and dreams are dismissed, mocked, or controlled.

Signs the situation is abusive (and needs a safety plan)

  • Any physical violence, or threats of it — to you, your children, or pets.
  • Controlling your money, movements, phone, or who you see.
  • Intimidation, stalking, or threats about what will happen if you leave.
  • Isolating you from family, friends, or your church community.

ACTYour honest inventory

Answer as plainly as you can. No one has to see this but you and God.

IMPORTANT

If anything in the abusive list is true for you, do not skip Module 3. Build your safety plan before you take visible steps toward leaving.

MODULE 2

The Decision: faith, mindset & permission to go

God did not call you to a life of constant survival. He called you to peace.

This is often the hardest module — not because of logistics, but because of the war inside. You may love this person. You may have history, children, or a faith community that complicates everything. Let’s gently untangle some of that.

UNDERSTANDFaith does not require self-destruction

Faithfulness and self-abandonment are not the same thing. Scripture calls us to love, but never to let ourselves be destroyed. You are one of God’s beloved daughters — your safety, your dignity, and your peace matter to Him. Leaving a relationship that is breaking you can be an act of faith, not a betrayal of it.

If you are wrestling with the spiritual weight of this decision, you do not have to carry it alone. A wise pastor, a faith-based counselor, or a domestic-violence advocate who respects your beliefs can help you hold both your convictions and your wellbeing.

UNDERSTANDThe fog of leaving

Toxic relationships create a mental fog — you doubt your memory, minimize what happened, and swing between “I have to go” and “Maybe I’m overreacting.” This is normal. It is also why we write things down. Your written words become an anchor on the days the fog rolls back in.

MODULE 3

Safety First: protecting yourself before you move

A good exit plan is a quiet plan. Protect yourself first; announce nothing you don’t have to.

This module matters most if there is any controlling, threatening, or violent behavior. Even in a relationship that is “just” toxic, discretion protects your peace and your leverage. The goal is simple: be ready before anyone knows you are getting ready.

UNDERSTANDWhy leaving is a sensitive moment

Control is the engine of many toxic and abusive relationships. The moment a controlling partner senses you slipping away, behavior can escalate. This is not a reason to stay — it is a reason to prepare carefully and to lean on people trained for exactly this.

MODULE 4

The Money Plan: building your freedom fund

Money is not the point of your freedom — but it is often the bridge to it.

Financial dependence is one of the top reasons women stay in relationships that hurt them. The aim is to quietly build enough resources and independence that leaving becomes possible — not someday, but on a real timeline you can see.

UNDERSTANDWhat ‘enough to leave’ really means

You do not need a fortune. You need enough to land safely and stay landed for a few months while you stabilize. A common target is three months of basic expenses — but some women leave with far less and rebuild from there. Start where you are; a small fund beats no fund.

MODULE 6

Where will you go: housing & logistics

You are not just leaving something. You are going toward a place that is finally yours.

Housing is usually the biggest piece of the puzzle, so give yourself options. There is no shame in any path — a friend’s spare room, a family member’s home, a women’s shelter, transitional housing, or your own lease. Each one is a doorway out.

UNDERSTANDYour housing options

Short-term landing spots

  • Staying with trusted family or friends while you stabilize.
  • Domestic violence shelters and transitional housing (often free and confidential).
  • Faith-based and community housing assistance programs.

Your own place

  • Renting a room, a studio, or an apartment in your name.
  • Income-based or subsidized housing if you qualify.
  • Splitting a place with a trusted roommate to lower costs.

ACTHousing readiness checklist

If you have children

  • Consider school zones and the least disruptive timing for them.
  • Think through childcare near your new home and workplace.
  • Keep their comfort items and routines as steady as you can.

MODULE 7

The Exit: executing your plan

Preparation turns a terrifying leap into a series of calm, ordered steps.

By now you have clarity, a fund forming, your documents gathered, and a place to land. This module is about the actual day (or days) of leaving — doing it as safely and smoothly as your situation allows.

UNDERSTANDChoose your exit style

  • The planned, calm exit — when it’s safe to leave openly, on a chosen day, with help lined up.
  • The quiet exit — leaving while the other person is out, when announcing it could escalate things.
  • The emergency exit — leaving immediately for safety, with your grab-and-go bag, and sorting the rest later.

There is no “correct” way. Safety chooses the style; your plan adapts to it.

MODULE 8

Legal Basics: protection, custody & your rights

Knowledge is leverage. You don’t need to be a lawyer — you need to know your options.

PLEASE NOTE

This module is general education, not legal advice. Laws vary widely by state and country. For your situation, talk to a family-law attorney or a free/low-cost legal aid office. Many DV organizations offer free legal advocates.

UNDERSTANDTools that may protect you

  • Protective / restraining orders — court orders that can require the other person to stay away. Often available quickly in emergencies.
  • Temporary custody & support — courts can set interim arrangements for children and finances while things are sorted.
  • Separation & divorce — the process and your rights to shared property and support depend on local law.

MODULE 9

Co-Parenting & boundaries after you leave

You ended the relationship. If there are children, you didn’t end the connection — so we make it as low-conflict as possible.

If you share children with the person you left, you’ll likely have ongoing contact. The aim is not friendship and not war — it’s a businesslike, boundaried arrangement that protects your peace and shields your kids from the conflict.

UNDERSTANDThe gray-rock approach

With a difficult or manipulative co-parent, many women find that giving very little emotional reaction — staying calm, brief, and factual, like a ‘gray rock’ — starves the conflict of fuel. You are not cold; you are simply no longer available for the drama.

ACTBuild boundaries that hold

Protecting your children’s hearts

  • Don’t speak badly about their other parent in front of them — even when it’s true.
  • Keep their routines steady; predictability helps them feel safe.
  • Reassure them often that none of this is their fault and that they are loved.
  • Watch for changes in mood, sleep, or behavior — consider child counseling if needed.
WHEN CONTACT ISN’T SAFE

If communication is being used to harass or control you, tell your attorney. Courts can order monitored apps, supervised exchanges, or, in serious cases, no contact.

MODULE 11

Healing: emotional & spiritual recovery

Leaving is the event. Healing is the journey. Give yourself the whole road.

You can be free and still be hurting. Healing from a toxic relationship is not linear — it loops, it surprises you, it asks for patience. The goal is not to never feel pain again. The goal is to slowly become whole, and more yourself than you’ve been in years.

UNDERSTANDWhat you may move through

  • Grief — even for something that hurt you. Mourning what you hoped it would be is normal.
  • Relief and guilt, often at the same time.
  • Rebuilding trust in your own judgment after being made to doubt it.
  • Rediscovering your identity, your voice, and your faith on your own terms.

ACTTend to yourself

BE GENTLE WITH THE TIMELINE

If you find yourself overwhelmed, numb, or unable to function for long stretches, please reach out to a professional. In the U.S. you can call or text 988 anytime. Asking for help is strength, not failure.
