It Was Always the Same Man

For a long time I thought I just had bad luck with men. A different face every time, a different name, sometimes a different bank account — and somehow the same ending waiting for me every single time.

It took me a while to say the real thing out loud: that wasn’t bad luck. It was a pattern. And the only person who could change it was me.

So I put the whole thing in one place. The mistakes I made that pushed good love away and pulled the wrong love in. How I finally walked away clean, without getting pulled back. What I had to change in myself. And what I taught myself to look for, before I ever hand a man my time, my body, or my peace again.

I’m not writing this as a woman who has it all figured out. I’m writing it as a woman who lived every page of this — owned her part, and chose different.

A NOTE BEFORE WE BEGIN

This Isn’t Shade. It’s a Mirror.

Everything in this part is something I did myself. I cooked for him. I moved in. I changed myself. I ignored every red flag God put right in front of my face. And I kept pouring into a man who had no intentions of giving me what I truly deserved.

I’m not here to judge anybody. I’m here because nobody told me — and I refuse to let another good woman waste her best years, her energy, and her heart on a man who’s just comfortable.

These five things, I thought, would keep him. They didn’t. Some of them did the exact opposite. Learn from my story, so it doesn’t have to become yours.

Five lessons · plus the one nobody says out loud

LESSON ONE

Don’t Date Outside Your Faith

“I thought love would be enough. Six years later, I found out it wasn’t.”

If your faith is a non-negotiable — and it should be — then the man you build with has to share it. Not just tolerate it. Not just show up on Easter. He needs to be truly aligned with where you’re going spiritually.

Unequally yoked isn’t just a Bible verse. It’s a warning. You can love someone deeply and still be completely incompatible in the ways that matter most. If he doesn’t pray, doesn’t go to church, doesn’t acknowledge God as the head of his home — you cannot drag him there. You cannot love him saved.

I saw every red flag in the beginning. Every. Single. One. He didn’t have the same relationship with God I did. But I told myself he’d change. Six years later, he hadn’t — and I’d spent secure, stable years being a spiritual misfit with a man who was never meant for my season.

Discernment is protection. When God shows you who someone is, believe Him — the first time.

LESSON TWO

Don’t Lose Yourself Trying to Please Him

“I started dressing how he liked. Wearing my hair how he liked. I forgot what I liked.”

Wear what you want. Style your hair how you love it. Wear your lashes, paint your nails, be cute and bold and feminine — whatever makes you feel like her. Don’t dim yourself for a man who hasn’t even put a ring on your finger yet.

Preference is for a husband. If he’s not your husband, he doesn’t get a say in your appearance, your wardrobe, or your identity. A man who tries to control how you look before he’s committed is practicing for something far more controlling later.

There’s a difference between dressing with class and dignity and shrinking yourself to make him comfortable. You can be classy, covered, and completely magnetic — but you have to stay yourself. When you lose who you are trying to keep him, you become a version of yourself that no longer feels good. And the scary part? He still might leave.

Your identity is yours. Protect it.

LESSON THREE

Don’t Give Him Wifey Treatment on a Girlfriend Budget

“I cooked like his mama. He ate like a king. And left like a guest.”

I am a cook. It’s in me. When I’m in love, I want to feed you, nurture you, show you I care. That’s a beautiful gift. But I gave it away too freely, too soon — and it became an expectation before it became a testimony.

Here’s the truth: you can dabble. A little here, a little there. Show him what’s possible. But full Sunday dinner every week? Meals prepped, kids fed, his plate waiting? That’s wifey behavior. And a man who gets wifey behavior without the wife title has zero urgency to upgrade you.

Especially if you have children — he’ll slide right into that routine and eat at your table like it’s a right, not a privilege. There are levels to what you offer and when you offer it.

Men value what they have to work toward. The table is set — but the invitation to dine forever comes with a ring.

LESSON FOUR

Don’t Move In Before the Ring

“I gave him all the benefits of a husband. He never had a reason to become one.”

I learned this the hard way. I moved in with a man I thought was going to marry me. I had his baby. I built a home, a routine, a life — inside of his plan. And I was a placeholder. A ready-made family, and none of that made him want to wife me up, because he already had everything marriage would offer without the commitment.

When you give a man full access — your home, your body, your babies, your routine — you become something he maintains, not something he pursues. There’s no shame in my story. But if I could do it over, I’d have protected my home. My space should have stayed mine — somewhere he had to earn a permanent invitation to.

A man who wants to marry you will find a way. He won’t need you to move in first to test it out. The commitment opens the door.

LESSON FIVE

Hold Your Standard Around Intimacy — And Mean It

“Once I gave that away, something shifted. And it wasn’t me getting a ring.”

I don’t care what culture says, or if everyone around you thinks the standard is outdated. Your body is sacred, and a man who truly respects you will respect your conviction around it — or remove himself. Either way, you win.

When you hold this standard, you communicate something powerful: I know my worth. My body is a covenant, not a consolation prize. That commands a different level of respect. But the moment it happens too soon — before commitment, before covenant — the pursuit can cool and the urgency can fade.

This only works when you mean it from a place of self-respect, not strategy. You are valuable, set apart, and worth waiting for. A man who won’t wait is telling you exactly who he is — before you’re in too deep.

BONUS · THE WHOLE TRUTH

Some Men Simply Still Want to Roam

“And they’ll make you think it’s your fault — so you keep fixing yourself while they keep playing.”

Here’s the one nobody wants to say out loud: sometimes it’s not about what you did wrong. Sometimes a man simply isn’t ready to be faithful to one woman. He wants options. He wants access. And he lacks the integrity to be honest about it.

So what does he do? He makes you the problem. He points at your insecurities, your communication, your past wounds — not because growth isn’t real, but because he wants you focused on fixing yourself while he keeps doing him. It’s manipulation wrapped in self-help language. Don’t fall for it.

Some men know exactly how good you are. They want you around — but they want her around too. They’ll string you along for years, giving just enough, so you never fully leave. Selfish enough to keep you, not committed enough to choose you. That quiet knowing in your spirit? Listen to it. It’s not insecurity. It’s wisdom.

Give yourself a timeframe. Decide how long you’re willing to invest in someone who isn’t moving toward you — then hold the line. Not as punishment. As protection.

PART THREE · WHAT I CHANGED

Why I Kept Choosing Him

Leaving was only half of it. The pattern was mine to break — so these are the five shifts that finally broke it.

Here’s the thread under all of it: I gave relationship-level investment before there was a relationship-level commitment. I played wife before there was a title. I poured in early and full, then spent the rest of it waiting for him to catch up to everything I’d already given. He rarely did — because I front-loaded it all and left him nothing to grow toward. I arrived at 100. He arrived at 40. And I called it love, because it was the only version I’d practiced.

Where do you arrive?

SHIFT 1I let the role match the title.

I stopped handing a man wife-energy and mother-level care before he’d committed to anything. That care is a response to commitment, not bait for it.

SHIFT 2I slowed my investment down.

The pace I set in the first 90 days taught him what he could have without trying. I learned to let him invest first — sometimes — and to match his consistency instead of out-running it.

SHIFT 3I stopped overriding the flags I already saw.

I was never blind. My rule now: the first time I catch myself explaining something away, that’s my signal to slow all the way down.

SHIFT 4I learned to receive.

Love had always been something I earned by doing. The hardest, best change was letting myself be chosen while I did less — and noticing the discomfort that came up was the old pattern talking, not the truth.

SHIFT 5I named the need I was really chasing.

Under every “type” was a need I’d never gotten met — to be chosen and kept for who I am, not for what I provide. I couldn’t get met what I wouldn’t say out loud. So I finally said it.

A pattern that had me over-giving that hard was guarding one fear: that if I wasn’t useful, I wouldn’t be kept. The over-giving felt like insurance against being left — but it was the very thing that got me left. I don’t have to earn a seat at my own table.

PART FOUR · WHAT I LEARNED TO LOOK FOR

Charm Isn’t Character

I used to read a man by his charm. Now I read him by his consistency — and I keep a list for the woman I’m still becoming.

My biggest mistake was reading a man by his charm, his words, or his potential. Charm is a performance. Words are cheap. Potential is a guess about a man who hasn’t shown up yet. I learned the hard way that character only shows up in two places: time, and inconvenience.

So now I watch what a man does when it costs him something — when it’s inconvenient, when I need him, when there’s nothing in it for him. And I watch across weeks, not nights. Anyone can perform consistency for a weekend. Almost nobody can fake it for three months.

The “boring” trap

A steady man used to feel boring to me — because my nervous system was wired for chaos, and calm read as flat. I almost walked away from peace because it didn’t feel familiar. But I had to learn the other side too: calm is not automatically safe. The quiet one can be hiding things. Only consistency over time tells the truth — which is exactly why I slow down now before I hand over access.

The skill I built wasn’t “pick boring men.” It was learning to tell demonstrated consistency from declared consistency — what a man proves over time versus what he promises in the moment.
